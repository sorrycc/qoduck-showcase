01 — The cut edge is a waist

The slice is a half disc, flat side up. That straight edge is the only horizontal in the whole object, so it becomes the one place a body can plausibly enter it: the waistline. The torso is seated into the notch at the centre of the cut face, where the photograph shows the pale core and its short strands.

Because the flat edge is the widest part, the skirt reads as a classical tutu plate seen slightly from above — wide at the hip, curving away below.

02 — The rind sets the hem, the hem sets the pose

At 390 units of radius against a 128-unit head, the curve lands just below the knee. That single measurement dictates everything downstream: only the calves can show, so the dancer stands en pointe on one leg with the other in a petit retiré, its shin crossing below the hem to the supporting ankle.

The right arm reaches out until the fingertips hover a hand's width above the rind's tip — the body's widest gesture is bounded by the fruit's widest point.

03 — Colour and light come from the flesh

The flesh runs #FFD85E → #F29200 from core to rind, the pith ring is a cream #FCF0C8, and the zest carries the photograph's dotted oil glands. The leotard is the one invented hue — blood orange — and its seams radiate from the waist core, continuing the same radial system the membranes run down into the skirt.

The figure is dark so the fruit can act as the room's only lamp: amber light climbs the calves, the underside of the jaw and the lower edge of the extended arm. Turn the bloom down and the dancer falls back into silhouette.

04 — Built to stand alone

One HTML file. Every mark is vector geometry — the vesicles are seeded, the gradients are declared, the grain is a filter. No photograph is embedded, linked or traced, and nothing is fetched from the network.

Exports carry the current state: the SVG downloads with the segment count, pith width, ink and backdrop you have dialled in, and the PNG renders at twice the artboard.
